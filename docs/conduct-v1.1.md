1. Scope. This code applies to every competitor, mentor and reserve for the whole of the JengaSec 2026 season, on the platform and at the venue.

2. Rules of engagement. Attack only the targets assigned to your team, inside the engagement window, through the access the organisers issue. Anything else -- other teams' cells, the platform itself, the organisers' infrastructure, the public internet -- is out of scope and is treated as an attack on the event.

3. Honesty. Every proposal, report and piece of evidence is your team's own work. Declare all use of AI tools in the AI-use statement. Do not share, sell or reuse credentials, and report any you come across by accident.

4. People. Treat other competitors, judges and organisers with respect. Harassment, intimidation and discrimination of any kind end a team's participation immediately.

5. Data. Treat any data you reach during the competition as confidential. Do not exfiltrate, publish or retain it beyond what the evidence rules require.

6. Decisions. Organisers' rulings on scope, scoring and conduct are final, subject only to the appeals process in the Entry Guide.

7. Your personal data. This section is the privacy notice for this competition. It sits under the Strathmore University Data Privacy Policy and the Data Protection Act, 2019, and tells you what we hold about you and why.

7.1 Who holds it. Strathmore University is the data controller, acting through the Strathmore Cybersecurity Club, which runs JengaSec. The full university policy is at https://strathmore.edu/privacy-policy/ and this notice does not replace it.

7.2 What we collect. Identification data: your name, date of birth, and the student or national ID document you upload. Contact information: your email address and, for captains and deputies, a phone number. Academic data: your institution, student number, programme and year of study. Campus access data: what the university needs to admit you to the venue. Communication data: the notifications this platform sends you and whether they were read. Technical data: IP addresses recorded in the platform's audit log when you sign in or act on a record. Consent and authorisation data: the fact and time of your acceptance of this code.

7.3 Why we collect it. To confirm you are eligible to compete and within the age band the Entry Guide sets. To form teams and verify rosters. To admit you to Strathmore on the days of the event. To judge and score your team's work, and to handle any appeal. To contact you about the competition. To keep the event and this platform secure.

7.4 On what basis. Your consent, for the identity document and for taking part at all -- you give it by accepting this code, and you may withdraw it, though withdrawing it means we can no longer verify your eligibility and your participation ends. Our legitimate interests, for operating the platform securely and keeping an audit trail. The necessity of performing our side of your participation, for everything the competition itself requires.

7.5 Who sees it. Organisers and judges, as their roles require. The service providers that run the platform for us: our database host, our file storage, and our email provider. Nobody else, except where the law requires disclosure. Partners and sponsors receive aggregate figures -- how many teams, how many institutions -- and never your personal details, unless you separately agree.

7.6 Where it is held. The platform's database, the uploaded documents and the email service are hosted outside Kenya. Those transfers are made under the safeguards section 8 of the university policy requires.

7.7 Your identity document. It is seen only by organisers, is used only to confirm your student status and age, and is deleted once your eligibility has been verified and the event has closed. It is never shown to judges, other competitors or partners.

7.8 How long we keep it. For the JengaSec 2026 season and the period afterwards needed to settle appeals, satisfy audit and report on the event. After that it is securely disposed of, except for anonymised figures that can no longer identify you, which we may keep indefinitely.

7.9 Your rights. Under the Data Protection Act, 2019 you may ask to be told how your data is used, to see the data we hold about you, to object to our processing of some or all of it, to have inaccurate data corrected, and to have false or misleading data deleted. Write to the university's Data Protection Officer at dpo@strathmore.edu. You also have the right to complain to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner.

7.10 Keeping it accurate. Please keep your own profile up to date. An incomplete or inaccurate record is the usual reason a registration cannot be verified or a competitor cannot be admitted to the venue.

Breaches are penalised under the schedule in the Entry Guide, up to disqualification.
